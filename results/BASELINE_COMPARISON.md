# Corrected Baseline Comparison

The thermal scenarios were recalculated using net radiative exchange rather than gross blackbody emission.

| Scenario | Old useful power (W) | Corrected useful power (W) | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Solar reference | 225.00 | 225.00 | 0% |
| 300 K thermal | 41.33 | 3.65 | -91.2% |
| 500 K thermal | 318.94 | 281.27 | -11.8% |
| Solar + 300 K thermal | 266.33 | 228.65 | -14.2% |

## Interpretation

The correction materially changes the low-temperature thermal case. A 300 K source is only about 6.85 K above the 293.15 K receiver, so most of its gross blackbody emission is not available as net radiative heat transfer.

This is an important constraint for the Radiocity concept: ambient-temperature radiation is not automatically a large recoverable energy source. A useful architecture must exploit temperature differences, favorable geometry, spectral selectivity, or another non-equilibrium mechanism.

The 500 K case remains substantial under the idealized unit-area, unit-view-factor assumptions, but real systems will introduce geometric, material, spectral, and conversion losses.

These results are a baseline, not an engineering performance claim.
